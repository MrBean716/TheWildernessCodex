The Endgame

What max-level PvP looks like and what's required to compete

Endgame PvP in OSRS is the most mechanically demanding combat in the game. Fights between max players last seconds, not minutes — a single misclick or missed prayer tick can cost you several hundred million GP worth of gear. The rewards are proportional: a single max-gear kill can yield 100M+ GP in loot.

This guide assumes you've read and practised the Beginner and Mid-Level guides. If you haven't, stop here. The mechanics introduced here — tribrid switching, Vengeance stacking, prayer flicking at tick precision — require the fundamentals to be automatic first.

What Changes at Endgame

  • Three combat styles — Melee, Ranged, and Magic are all used in a single fight. You switch between them to bypass protection prayers.
  • Vengeance — A spell that recoils ~75% of the next hit you take back at your attacker. Stacking this with a spec creates massive burst damage.
  • Gear switches — You swap 4–6 items at once in a single tick to change combat style. This requires F-key fluency and muscle memory.
  • Prayer reading — You watch your opponent's overhead prayer and switch your attack style to the one they're not protecting against.
  • Risk — Full endgame gear costs 500M–2B GP. One death is a catastrophic financial event. Risk management is as important as combat skill.
Do not bring max gear into the Wilderness until you are completely comfortable with every mechanic in this guide — practised in Clan Wars repeatedly. Losing a full Bandos/Ancestral/Twisted Bow setup is the kind of loss that takes months of gameplay to recover from.

Prerequisites

  • 99 Attack, 99 Strength, 99 Defence, 99 Ranged, 94+ Magic, 70 Prayer (Piety), 74 Prayer (Rigour), 77 Prayer (Augury)
  • Completed Lunar Diplomacy (for Vengeance)
  • Completed Desert Treasure (for Ancient Magicks / Ice Barrage)
  • F-key switching completely automatic — no conscious thought required
  • Reliable combo eating under pressure
  • Basic prayer flicking (introduced at end of mid-level guide)

Vengeance

The defining endgame mechanic — how to cast, time, and stack it

Vengeance is a Lunar Magic spell that, when active, recoils approximately 75% of the next hit you take back at your attacker. It has a 30-second cooldown between casts. At endgame, the entire meta revolves around setting up a Vengeance stack — taking a big hit while Vengeance is active, then immediately spec'ing.

Requirements

  • 94 Magic (minimum)
  • Completed Lunar Diplomacy quest
  • Active on the Lunar spellbook
  • 1 Astral Rune + 2 Earth Runes + 4 Water Runes per cast
Vengeance requires 94 Magic. This is a significant stat to train on a PvP account — it adds combat levels. Budget for this investment. Many endgame accounts train Magic specifically to unlock Vengeance because it is that powerful.

How Vengeance Works in a Fight

When Vengeance is active and your opponent hits you for, say, 40 damage — they immediately take 30 damage back (75% of 40). This is separate from your own attack. So on the tick they hit you for 40, they receive 30 recoil damage while simultaneously being hit by whatever spec or attack you fire. That's a combined 30 + spec damage in one burst — potentially 80–100+ total damage on a single tick.

Double Vengeance ("Double Veng")

Because Vengeance has a 30-second cooldown, you can cast it right before entering a fight — then after it triggers (when they hit you), cast it again immediately. The second cast is ready because 30 seconds have passed during the approach and early fight. This means you potentially get two Vengeance triggers in one fight.

The Veng + AGS stack is feared because no amount of combo eating can save you from 80+ instant damage. The victim has one tick to react — but the damage lands simultaneously, faster than any eating response. This is why top PKers pre-eat to stay near max HP at all times.

Hybridding & Tribriding

Using multiple combat styles in a single fight to bypass protection prayers

Experienced players use Protect from Melee and Protect from Missiles during fights. These block an entire combat style. The solution is to switch combat styles, forcing your opponent to change their prayer — or take unblocked damage if they can't switch fast enough.

Hybridding (Two Styles)

Hybridding means alternating between melee and ranged (or melee and magic). The most common hybrid setup is:

Watch your opponent's overhead prayer icon. As soon as you see it change to Protect from Melee, switch to ranged. The moment they switch to Missiles, switch back to melee. The goal is always to be attacking with the style they're not praying against.

A full style switch at endgame involves swapping weapon, helm, body, amulet, and sometimes ring — all in a single tick. This is 5 clicks in 0.6 seconds. It requires drilling in Clan Wars until it's completely automatic.

Tribriding (Three Styles)

Tribriding uses Melee, Ranged, and Magic in rotation. This is the highest-skill PvP style. Most endgame PKers open with Ice Barrage to freeze the opponent (preventing them from running), then switch to melee for maximum damage while they're frozen, then ranged when the opponent prays melee.

Tribriding is the hardest skill in OSRS PvP. Do not attempt it in real fights until you've drilled it hundreds of times in Clan Wars. A failed style switch in a real fight with 500M+ gear on the line is an extremely costly mistake.

Reading Overhead Prayers

At endgame, every player is watching their opponent's prayer icon constantly. The icons appear directly above their character's head:

  • Yellow sword icon — Protect from Melee active. Switch to ranged or magic.
  • Arrow/bow icon — Protect from Missiles active. Switch to melee or magic.
  • Lightning bolt icon — Protect from Magic active. Switch to melee or ranged.
  • No icon — No protection prayer. Attack with your strongest style (usually melee).

Advanced Prayer Flicking

The highest-skill technique in OSRS PvP — blocking attacks for free

At endgame, prayer flicking is not optional. It's the difference between lasting 5 ticks and lasting 50 in a fight. The concept: turn your protection prayer on exactly one tick before an opponent's hit lands, then turn it off immediately after. The hit is blocked. You spent almost no prayer points.

Done correctly, a single Prayer Potion can last an entire deep Wilderness trip. Done incorrectly, your prayer drains and you take full damage from everything.

The Mechanics

Attacks in OSRS have a travel time after the animation — the hit doesn't land on the same tick as the swing. For melee, the delay is 1 tick. For ranged, 2–3 ticks depending on distance. For magic, 1–5 ticks depending on spell.

You watch the opponent's attack animation, count the appropriate tick delay, and flick your prayer on that exact tick.

Melee prayer flick — 1-tick delay
Watch opponent swing animation
→
Count 1 tick (0.6s)
→
F3 → activate Protect Melee
→
Hit blocked (0 damage)
→
Immediately deactivate prayer
→
Repeat on next swing

Flicking Against a Hybrid

When your opponent is hybridding, you need to flick between two different protection prayers — catching melee attacks with Protect Melee, and ranged attacks with Protect Missiles, all in the same fight.

Hybrid prayer response
Opponent on Scythe
→
Flick Protect Melee
→
They switch to TBow

Mastering prayer flicking against a hybrid takes hundreds of hours. Learn single-style flicking first (just melee, in Clan Wars 1v1s). Don't try hybrid flicking in real fights until single-style flicking is 100% reliable.

Overhead Prayer Reading

While you're flicking your own prayers, your opponent is watching your prayers and switching styles accordingly. This creates a fast back-and-forth — they switch style, you switch prayer, they read your prayer change and switch back, you read theirs and follow. The speed of this exchange is what defines high-level PvP skill.

Record yourself fighting in Clan Wars and review the footage. You'll see exactly when you failed to flick, when your prayer switch was one tick late, and what openings you missed. Video review is how the top PKers improve — it's not just about practicing more, it's about practicing correctly.

Target Hunting

Finding and engaging high-value targets in the deep Wilderness

At endgame, PKing is as much about finding the right fights as winning them. The Wilderness is large. Knowing where to hunt, what targets are profitable, and how to engage without giving your opponent time to teleport is a learnable skill.

High-Value Target Types

  • PvMers in the Wilderness — players doing Chaos Fanatic, Crazy Archaeologist, Scorpia, and Callisto/Venenatis/Vet'ion carry significant loot. They're focused on the boss, not watching for PKers.
  • Revenant Cave farmers — the Rev Caves at level 17–40 Wilderness attract players farming valuable drops. High concentration of targets.
  • Wilderness Slayer players — completing Slayer tasks in the Wilderness while carrying full gear and accumulated loot. Often worth 50–100M kills.
  • Other max PKers — the highest-risk target type. These fights are pure skill tests. Losing is expensive; winning is profitable and deeply satisfying.

Engagement Strategy

Endgame PKers don't chase targets — they close the gap before their target realises they're there. Key approach principles:

  • Cast Vengeance during your approach, 30+ seconds before engaging
  • Activate Run on approach to close distance quickly
  • Drink your Super Combat and Ranging Potion while running in (saves fight ticks)
  • Open with Ice Barrage to freeze them if you're tribriding — prevents teleport and escape
  • Target players who are low HP from PvM activity — they're easier kills
The Wilderness Ditch cannot be crossed while in combat. If you freeze an opponent near the ditch, they cannot escape south — they're trapped in the Wilderness until the freeze wears off. This is a core endgame trapping strategy.

Looting Bag

Always bring a Looting Bag into the deep Wilderness. This bag stores up to 28 additional items — meaning you can collect full inventories of loot from multiple kills in one trip. Items in the Looting Bag are safe on death (they disappear, but so does the bag — you don't drop the contents). Fill it before trying to leave.

Risk Management

At endgame, a single death costs 200M–2B GP. Serious endgame PKers treat every trip like a calculated investment:

  • Never take gear you can't afford to replace twice over
  • Always know your escape route before committing to a fight
  • Never fight when your food or prayer is below 50% — you're disadvantaged
  • Log out after a major loss. Tilting leads to worse decisions and more losses.
Going deep into the Wilderness with 500M+ GP in gear and dying is not a learning experience — it's a financial catastrophe. Only risk gear you can genuinely afford to lose. Endgame PKing should be funded by wins, not by liquidating your bank.

Advanced Tips

What separates good endgame PKers from elite ones

The Elite PKer's Code

  • 01
    Pre-eat to 110+ HP before every fight. Anglerfish can overheal beyond your max HP. Starting a fight at 110 HP instead of 99 HP is survivng one extra hit. At endgame, that's the whole fight.
  • 02
    Never spec an opponent above 80 HP. An AGS spec at 90 HP is a gift — they eat once and nullify your entire spec bar. Wait until they're low and out of food.
  • 03
    Vengeance timing is more valuable than spec timing. A 30-recoil + 60-spec stack is 90 damage in one tick. A 60-spec alone is just 60. Always set up Veng before speccing.
  • 04
    Cast Vengeance while running toward your target. The 30-second cooldown makes every second count. Casting at engagement wastes the best 30 seconds of the fight.
  • 05
    Watch prayer as aggressively as you watch HP. Running out of prayer mid-fight is almost always lethal. Carry Sanfew Serums instead of Prayer Potions — they restore more prayer and cure poison simultaneously.
  • 06
    The TBow is your prayer bypass, not your primary weapon. Its role is to deal damage when opponents pray melee, not to replace the Scythe. Switching to it when they're not praying missiles wastes its purpose.
  • 07
    Know what their spec weapon is. If they have Dragon Claws equipped, expect a 4-hit burst when you're low. If it's AGS, expect a slow single hit. Pre-eating before their spec is possible changes the fight entirely.
  • 08
    Log out after a big loss, not after a loss. Immediate re-entry to the Wilderness after a death leads to tilted decisions and more losses. Cool down for 10 minutes. Come back with a clear head.
  • 09
    Clan Wars grinding never stops. Even elite PKers drill new switches, test spec timing, and practice prayer flicks in Clan Wars. The meta evolves. Keep practising.
  • 10
    Study your kills and deaths equally. Kills tell you what worked. Deaths tell you what doesn't. The fastest improvement comes from understanding what actually decided each fight, not assuming you won because you're good or lost because of bad luck.

Progression Path Summary

If you've read all three guides, your path looks like this:

  1. Beginner (40/70/40) — Learn combo eating, escape, DDS basics. Clan Wars first, Edgeville second.
  2. Mid-Level (75/99/75) — Abyssal Whip, F-key switches, prayer usage, more complex spec timing.
  3. Endgame (99/99/99) — You're here. Veng stacking, hybridding, prayer flicking at tick precision, high-value hunting.

Each guide builds directly on the last. Every mechanic learned at Beginner is still used at Endgame — they're just faster, more automatic, and happening in parallel with more complex decisions. The foundation never stops mattering.
