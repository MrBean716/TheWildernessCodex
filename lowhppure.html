Low-HP Pure

Deliberately minimised Hitpoints to stay at ultra-low combat bracket

A Low-HP Pure trains offensive stats (typically Strength to 99) while deliberately keeping Hitpoints as low as possible. Because HP contributes to combat level, every HP level avoided means staying in a lower combat bracket — fighting opponents with less HP, weaker gear, and fewer food items.

The Low-HP Pure is distinct from the Baby Pure in intentionality: where a Baby Pure is simply an early-progression account, a Low-HP Pure is a deliberate design choice maintained throughout development. The player specifically chooses training methods that grant minimal HP XP to preserve the combat level advantage.

Hitpoints XP is gained alongside every combat XP earn. There is no way to train combat skills without gaining HP XP — but the ratio can be minimised. NMZ Absorption gives 4:1 Strength to HP XP, significantly reducing HP gains vs normal combat.

HP Management

Exactly how to minimise HP gains and why it matters

Why HP Level Matters

HP contributes 0.5 per level to combat (above level 10). Going from 10 HP to 50 HP costs 20 combat levels' worth of HP contribution. A Strength pure at 99 Strength but 10 HP would have a combat level around 35. The same account at 70 HP has combat level around 60. That 25-combat-level difference puts you in completely different brackets.

The NMZ Absorption Method

NMZ (Nightmare Zone) with Absorption potions: you deal damage to yourself via NMZ monsters while absorbing the hits. HP XP is gained per damage dealt — but using the Absorption method you're spending Absorption potions to avoid taking damage, meaning fewer HP XP events per training session.

The exact ratio: NMZ Absorption gives approximately 4 Strength XP per 1 HP XP — meaning 99 Strength via NMZ results in significantly lower HP than 99 Strength via regular combat.

Rockshell Plate and other similar items do NOT help reduce HP gains — that's a common misconception. The only thing that affects HP gain rate is your training method and the HP XP per action of that method.

Gear

Same as equivalent pure builds — gear choice doesn't change with HP level

A Low-HP Pure uses the same gear as the equivalent 1-Def pure at the same Attack level. The HP level affects combat level and bracket — not gear requirements or choices. A Low-HP G Maul Pure wears Obsidian set + Berserker Necklace, same as a standard Obby Mauler.

Rotations

Same as equivalent pure builds — only HP level differs

The Low-HP Pure uses identical rotations to its equivalent non-low-HP version. The only meaningful difference in combat is the lower HP pool — which makes combo eating more critical and the Anglerfish overheal more impactful.

Critical difference — combo eat threshold
At 30 HP base: combo eat at 15 HP→At 50 HP base: combo eat at 25 HP→Adjust threshold to your HP pool
With 35 HP base, your combo eat threshold drops to around 15 HP — leaving almost no margin for error. Anglerfish overheal to 57 HP before every fight is absolutely non-negotiable at this HP level.

Tips

  • 01
    NMZ Absorption is the only viable training method for Low-HP builds. Normal combat gives 3× more HP XP per Strength level. Pest Control is moderate. NMZ Absorption is the only method that seriously minimises HP gains.
  • 02
    Anglerfish overheal is non-negotiable, not optional. At 35–40 HP base, a single spec from most opponents is lethal. The overheal provides the only margin between surviving and dying.
  • 03
    The bracket advantage is the entire point. You fight people 10–15 CB levels above you who don't expect your damage output. Once opponents know you're a Low-HP Pure and start playing accordingly, the advantage shrinks.
  • 04
    Combo eat threshold scales with your HP. At 40 HP, eat below 15 HP. At 50 HP, eat below 20 HP. Never let HP drop near your base level — the margin for a kill shot below your base is zero.
  • 05
    Track your HP gains when training. Periodically check your HP level vs your Strength level as you train. If HP is growing faster than expected, you're using a training method with poor HP:Str ratio.
