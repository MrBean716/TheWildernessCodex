So You Want to PvP

Understanding the basics before you enter the Wilderness

PvP in Old School RuneScape is a high-stakes, skill-based combat system where players fight each other in designated zones — primarily the Wilderness and Bounty Hunter. If you die in the Wilderness, you lose your items. That risk is what makes every fight meaningful.

The beginner build (40/70/40) is the right starting point because you can get it quickly, the gear is cheap, and you'll learn all the core mechanics without risking expensive equipment. Once you've learned here, everything carries forward to higher-level builds.

Why 40/70/40?

  • 40 Attack unlocks the Dragon Scimitar — fast, accurate, cheap. The best weapon at this level range.
  • 70 Strength gives enough damage to threaten opponents without costing many combat levels.
  • 40 Defence unlocks Rune armour — the best non-member budget armour set. Enough to tank a few hits.
  • The build costs almost nothing to gear. Losses don't hurt your bank.
The Wilderness uses a level range system. At level 1–10 Wilderness, you can only be attacked by players within 3–10 combat levels of yours. Starting in low-level Wilderness means you face similarly-levelled opponents, not maxed accounts.

What You'll Learn Here

  • How game ticks work and why they matter in every fight
  • Combo eating — the most important beginner skill
  • Prayer basics: when to turn Protect from Melee on
  • How to escape a fight you're losing
  • Skulling rules and how to avoid losing everything on death

The Game Tick — Foundation of Everything

OSRS runs on a 0.6-second game tick. Every action — attacking, eating, moving, clicking prayers — resolves on tick boundaries. This is why you can eat food on the same tick you're attacked (it can save you), and why switching gear mid-fight is possible. Understanding ticks is what separates a button-masher from a player making real decisions.

The Dragon Scimitar attacks every 4 ticks (2.4 seconds). Between attack ticks, you have windows where you can eat, switch prayers, or switch equipment without losing any damage. Learning to use these windows is everything.

Spend your first hour in Clan Wars, not the Wilderness. Zero risk, free food respawn, real combat experience. Drill eating and prayer activation until you're not thinking about it — just doing it.

Spec Weapon: Dragon Dagger (P++)

Even at this level, the Dragon Dagger (P++) is your finishing weapon. Its special attack fires two rapid hits at 25% spec bar each. Two clicks = four hits total. The poison stacks on top for ongoing damage after the fight ends.

→

→

At level 70 Strength, each DDS spec hit can reach around 14–18 damage. Two full specs (4 hits) can deal up to ~60 damage total — enough to kill many opponents at this combat bracket from 40+ HP if your rolls are good.

What NOT to Bring

  • Don't bring your best gear to your first fights. If you die with Barrows armour as a beginner, that's a painful lesson. Learn the mechanics in cheap gear first.
  • Don't bring a Fire Cape unless you're confident in your escape game. The Wilderness is risky — an Obsidian Cape works fine.
  • Don't bring more than you're willing to lose. At beginner level, aim to risk under 50k GP total.

Combo Eating — Learn This First

Karambwan has a unique 0-tick eat mechanic. You can click a shark and immediately click a karambwan on the same game tick — your character processes both near-simultaneously. The result is roughly +32 HP in a single tick. This is your emergency panic button.

Combo Eat — Standard Sequence
HP drops below 25
→
Click Shark
→
Immediately click Karambwan
→
+32 HP in 1 tick

The key is clicking the Karambwan very quickly after the Shark — ideally within the same client tick. It takes practice. Don't expect to nail it in your first fight. Drill it in Clan Wars until your hands know the rhythm.

Never eat two regular foods in a row expecting them to act like a combo eat. Only Karambwan has the 0-tick property. Two sharks takes two full ticks — during which you can take lethal damage.

Beginner Inventory

🐟 10× Swordfish or Shark  |  🐟 4× Karambwan  |  ⚗️ 1× Super Strength (4 dose)  |  ⚗️ 1× Super Attack (4 dose)  |  ⚗️ 1× Stamina Potion (1 dose)  |  🗡️ Dragon Dagger (P++)  |  Remaining: extra food
Keep sharks in the top row of your inventory and Karambwans right next to them. When you need to combo eat, your eyes and hands need to find the food in under a second. Organisation matters.

Prayer Basics

If you have 43+ Prayer, you can use Protect from Melee. When activated, melee attacks against you deal 0 damage. This is enormously powerful but costs Prayer points — which run out without a Prayer Potion.

Basic prayer usage — beginner
Engaged in melee fight
→
F3 → Activate Protect Melee
→
Melee damage blocked
→
Watch prayer orb — use pot before it hits 0
If your opponent is using Ranged against you, Protect from Melee does nothing. You need Protect from Missiles for ranged attacks, and Protect from Magic for mage. Pay attention to what's hitting you and match accordingly.

Core Mechanics

The foundational rules of OSRS PvP every beginner must understand

The Skull System

Attacking another player in the Wilderness first gives you a skull — a small flame icon above your head. If you die while skulled, you lose all your items (normally you keep your 3 most valuable). This is how people lose their entire bank.

  • Unskulled death — you keep 3 most valuable items (+ Protect Item prayer saves a 4th)
  • Skulled death — you lose everything unless you use Protect Item to save one item
  • The skull lasts 20 minutes or until you leave the Wilderness
As a beginner, only skull when you're deliberately hunting a specific target. Walking near Edgeville unskulled means even if you get killed, you keep most of your gear. Never skull by accident by clicking the wrong player.

Wilderness Level Range

The deeper you go in the Wilderness, the wider the combat level range of players who can attack you:

  • Level 1–5 Wilderness — only players within 3–5 combat levels can attack you. Safest zone. Start here.
  • Level 10 Wilderness — players within 10 combat levels. Still fairly safe for beginners.
  • Level 20+ Wilderness — larger ranges. Higher-level accounts can reach you. Be cautious.
  • Level 30+ Wilderness — anyone within 30 levels. Full mains can find you at level 60 combat.

Teleporting Out

You can teleport out of the Wilderness from level 30 and below using standard spells (Varrock, Lumbridge, Falador teleports). From level 20 and below, your Amulet of Glory works. Above level 30, you cannot teleport — you're trapped.

Edgeville Wilderness starts at level 1–3. Keep your fights in levels 1–10 as a beginner. You can always teleport home in an emergency. The Wilderness level is shown in the top-right corner of your minimap.

The Protect Item Prayer

If you're about to die and can only use one prayer, use Protect Item. It saves your single most valuable item on death — even while skulled. At beginner level, this means you'll save your Dragon Scimitar no matter what.

Eating Without Losing Attack Ticks

You can eat food between attack ticks without delaying your next hit. The key is timing: click food in the tick immediately after your character's swing animation — not before. If you eat on the same tick as you'd attack, you delay the attack. Learning this timing means you maintain full damage output while healing.

Tips & Common Mistakes

What separates a beginner from a capable PKer

The 10 Beginner Rules

  • 01
    Always pot before fighting. Super Strength and Super Attack are not optional. Forgetting them costs you ~25% damage output for the entire fight. This kills people.
  • 02
    Don't eat above 30 HP unless it's the last tick before a combo eat. Eating at 47 HP heals 13 real HP. The rest is wasted if you were near full. Patience with food wins fights.
  • 03
    Karambwans are not optional. Bring them every single trip. The combo eat is a core survival mechanic, not an advanced technique. Learn it in week one.
  • 04
    Stamina Potion every trip, no exceptions. It costs 2k GP. It saves a 200k GP gear set. There is no reason to leave without one dose.
  • 05
    Save your DDS spec for the close. Speccing a full-HP opponent does nothing useful. They eat through it. Wait until they're low and food-less — then spec.
  • 06
    Recognise unwinnable fights instantly and run. Dharok's set, max-gear main, 99 Range with Twisted Bow — none of these are fights you can win. Run the moment you see them.
  • 07
    Stay in level 1–10 Wilderness until you're confident. The further you go, the worse the matchups. Low Wilderness keeps you in manageable fights while you learn.
  • 08
    Protect Item if you're about to die. It saves your best item on death. At your gear level, that's the Dragon Scimitar. Always worth using over Protect from Melee when death is certain.
  • 09
    Clan Wars first, Wilderness second. Zero risk, infinite practice. Every mechanic in this guide can be drilled in Clan Wars. Don't skip it.
  • 10
    Deaths are the curriculum, not failures. You will die. Many times. The PKers you admire died exactly as much learning. Note what killed you and fix it. That's the whole process.

Mistakes That Kill Beginners

Eating at 60+ HP. You're healing 5–10 real HP while spending a full shark. Wait until you actually need the healing — below 25–30 HP.
Forgetting to pot. If you engage without Super Strength, you're doing roughly 25% less max damage for the entire fight. This consistently loses fights you should win.
Staying to fight Dharok's. The Dharok's special grows stronger as the player's HP drops. Players deliberately stay near 1 HP for max damage. Do not engage. Run.
Skulling accidentally. Right-clicking the wrong player and attacking gives you a skull. Now if you die to anything in the next 20 minutes, you lose everything. Pay attention to who you're clicking.
Going past level 20 Wilderness as a beginner. Higher-level accounts start appearing. You can't teleport above level 30. You get trapped. Stay in the shallow end until you're comfortable.

Where to Start

The safest progression path from first fight to confident PKer

Step 1: Clan Wars

Accessed through the portal in Ferox Enclave (south of the Wilderness). Choose the Free-for-All portal. No risk. Food respawns. You can fight endlessly. This is where you drill combo eating, prayer switching, and spec weapon timing until they're automatic.

Spend at least a few hours here before going to the Wilderness. It is not optional. Every hour you spend here saves you multiple full gear sets in real Wilderness.

Step 2: Bounty Hunter

Located north of Edgeville. Accept an assignment and you're paired with a target at a similar combat level. Controlled matchmaking, structured stakes. Use budget gear (50k GP risk max). Your losses here will teach you something and you won't lose much.

Step 3: Edgeville Wilderness (Level 1–5)

Your first real Wilderness experience. The Edgeville bank is 15 seconds away. If you run out of food or feel overwhelmed, sprint back and restock in under a minute. At level 1–5, combat level ranges are tight — you'll face similar opponents.

Fight here until you've had 20+ fights and feel like you know what you're doing. That might take an hour or a week — it doesn't matter. Move forward when you're ready, not before.

Step 4: Edgeville Wilderness (Level 5–15)

Pushing slightly deeper. More players, slightly wider ranges, higher chance of running into a more geared opponent. Keep your risks below 100k GP. You should now be comfortable with combo eating and escaping.

The Ferox Enclave pool (inside the enclave) restores both your HP and Prayer to full for free. Use it before every Wilderness trip to start at peak health. It's inside the Wilderness border but you're safe from attack inside the Enclave.
Never go past level 30 Wilderness as a beginner. You lose teleport access and face players who regularly hunt low-level accounts deep in the Wilderness for easy kills.
